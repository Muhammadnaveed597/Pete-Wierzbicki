Your new About section

The short version of what changes

Your current About is rigorous, but it opens inside the theory and assumes the reader already accepts the premise. The new version keeps your architectural voice and puts your record in front: four decades of building systems where drift, identity, and reproducibility were not abstract ideas. It also adds what is missing today: who this is for, what the architecture establishes, and a clear path to the deeper material.

I have spent four decades building systems that were not allowed to drift. At Atari and Epyx, I wrote engine-level code when memory, determinism, and real-time constraints were the architecture. At Electronic Arts, I spent 13 years as the sole engineering authority for a platform that kept an entire back catalog buildable, verifiable, and commercially alive, a republishing system that enabled roughly $60 million in annual revenue. I reconstructed development environments where one missing dependency meant a finished game could never be rebuilt. In a Top Secret data center, I was responsible for the integrity, lineage, and lifecycle of satellite data that scientific research depended on. That work taught me something the current AI conversation keeps circling without naming: when a system cannot say what it is, where it came from, or what governs it, execution is already unstable. Most intelligent systems today are built execution-first. They act, then try to govern with filters, patches, and post-hoc controls. That works until systems become autonomous, distributed, and long-lived. Then the ordering itself creates the instability: drift, mutation, and lost authority boundaries. U4CE is my answer to that. It is a Universal 4D Cognitive Engine: a non-executing cognitive substrate that sits beneath the stack and resolves meaning, identity, and lineage before execution. Not a model. Not a framework. An architectural structure that separates semantic identity, reasoning context, and execution so cognition remains predictable, auditable, and durable as models, domains, and infrastructure evolve. What it establishes, before anything executes: Identity and lineage. Context and evolutional context. Continuity. Governed cognition. Admissibility and eligibility. Who it is for: Organizations building or governing AI in regulated, high-stakes, and long-lived domains, where "we will add guardrails later" is not an acceptable architecture. The canonical architecture is complete and patent-pending. It is designed to integrate with existing systems and future technologies without rebuild, replacement, or disruption. Deeper technical material is shared selectively. If you are working on AI governance, cognitive infrastructure, or long-lived autonomous systems and want the canonical overview, message me here.

A complete first post, written in your voice

I spent part of 13 years at Electronic Arts reconstructing build environments from archival media. A finished game, delivered years earlier, had to be rebuilt from source. Toolchains, libraries, licensing systems, environment variables, hundreds of dependencies. If one was missing or wrong, the executable would not build, and a title that generated real revenue was unrecoverable. That work changes how you see the current AI conversation. Every week I read that some system "drifted," lost track of context, confused authority, or could not explain why it produced an output. The industry responds with more guardrails, more filters, more monitoring. Those are downstream responses. In my reconstruction work, downstream was too late. If the environment that produced the artifact was not preserved and governed, the artifact was gone. The only durable solution was upstream: identity and lineage, established before execution, preserved across change. AI is now producing artifacts and actions at a scale no archive was designed for, with far less control over the environments that produce them. That is not a capability problem. It is the same problem I spent a career solving, at a new layer. This is why I am building U4CE: a non-executing cognitive substrate that establishes identity, lineage, and governed context before execution, so intelligence remains predictable, auditable, and durable as it scales. The question is not whether AI needs more capability. It is whether we preserve the conditions that make capability trustworthy. What is the earliest point in a system's life where you think governance should begin?
